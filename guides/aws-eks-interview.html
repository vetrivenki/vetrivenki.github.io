Incident 1: Pods crash or restart

Situation: Users experience errors after a rollout; pods show CrashLoopBackOff or OOMKilled.

Investigation: Check deployment history, describe/events, previous logs, exit codes, readiness/liveness probes,

memory trends and downstream dependencies.

Resolution: If OOMKilled, restore service by reverting or safely increasing memory based on evidence; if application

failure, roll back or fix config/secrets. Verify ready replicas, errors and latency.

Prevention: Add memory dashboards, realistic limits, load tests and release health gates.

Interview delivery: Explain customer impact, what you personally checked, the evidence supporting the root cause,

how service was restored, and the measurable outcome.

Incident 2: Karpenter cannot provision nodes

Situation: HPA requests more pods, but pods remain Pending and capacity does not increase.

Investigation: Inspect scheduling events, Karpenter logs, NodePool/EC2NodeClass, taints, instance requirements,

EC2 capacity, service quotas and subnet IPs.

Resolution: Correct restrictive instance selection, missing permissions or subnet constraints; allow eligible instance

families and validate NodeClaims, nodes and pod scheduling.

Prevention: Test scale-out, diversify capacity choices and alert on pending pods and low subnet IPs.

Interview delivery: Explain customer impact, what you personally checked, the evidence supporting the root cause,

how service was restored, and the measurable outcome.

Incident 3: ALB / ingress 502, 503 or 504

Situation: Users cannot reliably reach healthy-looking services.

Investigation: Inspect ALB target health and reason codes, ingress/controller logs, service endpoints, target ports,

health-check path, TLS and network controls.

Resolution: Correct the actual fault (for example health-check mismatch, missing endpoints, port mismatch or

backend timeout); verify healthy targets and end-to-end requests.

Prevention: Standardize health checks, rollout verification and network dashboards.

Interview delivery: Explain customer impact, what you personally checked, the evidence supporting the root cause,

how service was restored, and the measurable outcome.

Incident 4: AZ disruption and capacity loss

Situation: An AZ becomes unavailable; nodes and pods disappear or lose connectivity.

Investigation: Check AWS Health, EKS nodes, ALB targets, AZ spread, pod scheduling, Karpenter capacity, persistent

volumes and database status.

Resolution: Keep traffic on healthy AZs; restore replicas where capacity and storage permit; verify database failover

and representative transactions.

Prevention: Spread workloads, test AZ evacuation, maintain headroom and validate RTO/RPO.

Interview delivery: Explain customer impact, what you personally checked, the evidence supporting the root cause,

how service was restored, and the measurable outcome.

Incident 5: Terraform change causes production impact

Situation: A run partially applies or plans unexpected resource replacement.

Investigation: Freeze subsequent applies; inspect run logs, current plan, state, AWS reality, module inputs and recent

changes.

Resolution: Correct configuration, reconcile drift or state through supported commands, re-plan and peer-review;

apply only after a safe recovery plan.

Prevention: Enforce plan review, policy checks, versioned modules and rollback/migration procedures.

Interview delivery: Explain customer impact, what you personally checked, the evidence supporting the root cause,

how service was restored, and the measurable outcome.

Conflict 1: Developer blames infrastructure

Situation: A developer attributes errors to EKS; telemetry suggests an application-side issue.

Action: Invite joint triage, compare pod events, app logs and traces, agree on facts and jointly test the fix.

Illustrative result: Restored service and established a shared troubleshooting checklist.

Interview phrase: I avoided debating ownership and aligned the team around evidence.

Conflict 2: Manager requests risky production release

Situation: Business deadline conflicts with an incomplete Terraform/EKS validation.

Action: Explain specific blast radius and options; propose phased rollout, go/no-go criteria and rollback.

Conflict 3: Engineer disagrees on Terraform module design

Situation: One engineer favors per-environment copies; another favors reusable modules.

Action: Listen, compare maintainability and isolation, build a proof of concept, document trade-offs.

Illustrative result: Adopted an agreed modular pattern with environment-specific inputs.

Interview phrase: I used a small experiment rather than personal preference.

Conflict 4: Security rejects broad IAM access

Situation: An application requires access but the requested role violates least privilege.

Action: Review required API calls, use CloudTrail/Access Analyzer, narrow policies, validate in test.

Illustrative result: Delivered required access with reviewed permissions.

Interview phrase: I treated security as a design partner.

Conflict 5: Manager assigns competing urgent priorities

Situation: EKS upgrade, incident remediation and Terraform delivery overlap.

Action: Rank by customer impact, security urgency and dependencies; propose sequencing and milestone updates.

Illustrative result: Created explicit priorities and reduced unplanned context switching.

Interview phrase: I made trade-offs visible and asked for a clear priority decision.
